# Analyzing Evolutionary Conservation of Gene Regulation Through Bioinformatics

This repository contains the documents for a Bioinformatics coursework assignment as part of the Genomics in Molecular Medicine course. The coursework is divided into three parts, each focusing on different aspects of bioinformatics analysis.

## Part 1: Nuclear Retinoic Acid Receptors (RARs)

Part 1 explores the dynamic and combinatorial control of gene expression by nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RARs). The main tasks include:

- Collecting and aligning RAR protein sequences from various vertebrate species
- Generating a phylogenetic tree and analyzing the conservation of key phosphorylation sites
- Comparing the RAR paralogue RARB and investigating its potential activation mechanism
- Analyzing the impact of mutations in the dystrophin gene (DMD) and evaluating potential gene therapy strategies

## Part 2: Protein Sequence Analysis

Part 2 focuses on identifying and gathering information about a specific protein sequence. The objectives are:

- Identifying the species and gene to which the given sequence belongs
- Exploring alternative transcripts and paralogs
- Investigating ortholog genes in other vertebrates and constructing a phylogeny
- Identifying potential regulatory and promoter regions based on epigenetic marks
- Summarizing the phenotypic associations and findings related to the identified gene and its paralogs

## Part 3: Gene Evolution Analysis

In Part 3, the coursework aims to describe the evolution of a provided gene sequence. The tasks include:

- Identifying the gene of interest, its function, and expression patterns
- Illustrating the conservation of synteny across different species
- Discussing the conservation of synteny about vertebrate evolution
- Identifying related genes (paralogs) and interpreting the observations in the context of vertebrate evolution

## Results

The coursework yielded the following key results:

- **Conducted analysis of retinoic acid receptor alpha (RARA) across 10 vertebrate species using NCBI, Ensembl, and BLAST:**
  - Identified conserved Ser77 and Ser369 phosphorylation sites
  - Compared Human RARA and RARB protein sequences with Blast2seq, finding 78.5% sequence homology

- **Analyzed the dystrophin gene (DMD):**
  - Aligned wild-type and mutated DMD gene versions using Clustal Omega to highlight deletion impacts

- **Investigated the OPN1LW gene:**
  - Used Ensembl's BLAT tool to identify and visualize OPN1LW gene and its transcripts
  - Found 96.43% identity with paralogs
  - Applied UCSC genome browser to identify regulatory regions, finding high H3K4Me1, transcription factor clusters, DNase clusters, and H3K27Ac signatures

- **Analyzed NCAM2 in Homo sapiens:**
  - Used NCBI BLAST and Ensembl for analysis
  - Highlighted synteny conservation with Genomicus
  - Created a gene tree using TreeFam
