# Analyzing-Evolutionary-Conservation-of-Gene-Regulation-Through-Bioinformatics

This repository contains the documents for a Bioinformatics coursework assignment as part of the Genomics in Molecular Medicine course. The coursework is divided into three parts, focusing on different aspects of bioinformatics analysis.

## Part 1

In Part 1, the coursework explores the dynamic and combinatorial control of gene expression by nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RARs). The main tasks include:

1. Collecting and aligning RAR protein sequences from various vertebrate species
2. Generating a phylogenetic tree and analyzing the conservation of key phosphorylation sites
3. Comparing the RAR paralogue RARB and investigating its potential activation mechanism
4. Analyzing the impact of mutations in the dystrophin gene (DMD) and evaluating potential gene therapy strategies

## Part 2

Part 2 focuses on identifying and gathering information about a specific protein sequence. The objectives are:

1. Identifying the species and gene to which the given sequence belongs
2. Exploring alternative transcripts and paralogs
3. Investigating ortholog genes in other vertebrates and constructing a phylogeny
4. Identifying potential regulatory and promoter regions based on epigenetic marks
5. Summarizing the phenotypic associations and findings related to the identified gene and its paralogs

## Part 3

In Part 3, the coursework aims to describe the evolution of a provided gene sequence. The tasks include:

1. Identifying the gene of interest, its function, and expression patterns
2. Illustrating the conservation of synteny across different species
3. Discussing the conservation of synteny about vertebrate evolution
4. Identifying related genes (paralogs) and interpreting the observations in the context of vertebrate evolution


## Results

* Conducted analysis of retinoic acid receptor alpha (RARA) across 10 vertebrate species using NCBI, Ensembl, and BLAST, identifying conserved Ser77 and Ser369 phosphorylation sites and compared Human RARA and RARB protein sequences with Blast2seq, finding 78.5% sequence homology.
* Aligned wild-type and mutated DMD gene versions using Clustal Omega to highlight deletion impacts and used Ensembl's BLAT tool to identify and visualise OPN1LW gene and its transcripts, finding 96.43% identity with paralogs.
* Applied UCSC genome browser to identify regulatory regions for OPN1LW, finding high H3K4Me1, transcription factor clusters, DNase clusters, and H3K27Ac signatures and analysed NCAM2 in Homo sapiens with NCBI BLAST and Ensembl, highlighting synteny conservation with Genomicus, and created a gene tree using TreeFam.
